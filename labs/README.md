# Progressive Labs — Five Levels

> The top-level ladder of practical labs. Each level is a *capstone
> circuit* for its third of the course: it assumes the module labs
> are done and chains their skills into one longer, realistic piece
> of work. Difficulty climbs by **autonomy**, not by trivia — Level 5
> hands you a broken server and a deadline, exactly like real life.

| Level | Name | Assumes | Time | Theme |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | [Beginner — The First Session](level-1-beginner.md) | M01–M07 | 60–90 min | move, look, organize |
| 2 | [Intermediate — The Analyst's Day](level-2-intermediate.md) | M08–M13 | 2 h | text, pipelines, identity |
| 3 | [Advanced — The Automation Bench](level-3-advanced.md) | M16–M19 | 3 h | script it, schedule it, back it up |
| 4 | [Administrator — The Fleet Exercise](level-4-administrator.md) | M20–M25 | 4 h | services, net, security, logs |
| 5 | [Data Science Server — The Full Circle](level-5-ds-server.md) | M26–M31 | full day | the whole stack on one machine |

## Rules of the ladder

1. **Evidence, always.** Every level starts `script level-N.log`
   and ends with a findings file. An artifact without evidence
   doesn't exist.
2. **Break on purpose.** From Level 3 up, each lab includes a
   break-and-repair section: you damage your own build, then fix it
   from symptoms only.
3. **No internet crutch.** Solutions live in your notes and the
   module labs — searching is allowed from Level 4 only, and must be
   noted in your log (real operators cite sources too).
4. **Safety contract** (all levels): your own VM only; destructive
   commands only inside the lab's stated scratch trees; sudo only
   where the sheet says so.

## Grading (if used for credit)

Each level: 10 points — method rubric per sheet, result-only caps at
6. Levels 1–2 are checkpointed in-lab; 3–5 are submitted as evidence
bundles. The ladder deliberately mirrors the
[practical exam](../assessments/practical/practical-exam.md) style so
exam week is a rehearsal, not a surprise.
