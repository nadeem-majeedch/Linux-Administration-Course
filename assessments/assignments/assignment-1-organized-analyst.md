# Assignment 1 — The Organized Analyst

> Covers M05–M13 (terminal, files, paths, text tools, permissions) ·
> 5% of course grade · due end of week 6 · individual work.
> Environment: your own Ubuntu VM.

## The story

You inherited a messy analysis folder from a departing intern. Your
job is to bring it to professional standard — organized, documented,
permissioned — and to prove every transformation with a transcript.

## Setup

Download the starter bundle from the course portal
(`a1-starter.tar.gz`) and unpack it:

```console
$ tar xzf a1-starter.tar.gz -C ~
$ cd ~/a1-starter
```

The bundle contains: `messy/` (47 files: mixed `.csv`, `.txt`,
`.log`, duplicates, one file with spaces in its name, one
zero-byte file, three `.csv` files that are actually tab-separated),
plus `INSTRUCTIONS.txt` from the intern (deliberately vague).

## Part A — Triage & reorganize (30%)

1. Produce an inventory of `messy/`: total size, file-type census
   (extension → count → total bytes), the three largest files.
   Save as `reports/inventory.txt`.
2. Reorganize into this layout **without deleting anything**:

```text
clean/
├── data/        all real .csv files (tab-separated ones normalized to commas)
├── logs/        all .log files, sorted into by-month subdirectories
│                by their filename dates (files named like log-2026-01-*)
├── docs/        .txt files that are not logs (state your rule in README)
└── README.md    your reorganization rules — a reader must be able
                to re-derive your tree from your rules
```

3. Create `clean/MANIFEST.md`: every moved file, old path → new
   path. Generated by pipeline, not typed.

## Part B — Find the anomaly (30%)

The intern left a poisoned file. Using pipelines (show them):

1. Identify any `.csv` in `data/` whose column count is inconsistent
   with its header row (one of them is). Save the filename and the
   offending line to `reports/anomaly.txt`.
2. Detect duplicate *content* across all `.csv` files (same data,
   different names) and record the pair in `reports/duplicates.txt`.
3. Write a one-paragraph verdict in `reports/verdict.md`: which file
   should be quarantined and why your method guarantees you didn't
   miss another.

## Part C — Permission it like a team folder (40%)

1. Create group `analysts`, add your user to it.
2. `clean/` becomes a team tree: group `analysts`, all directories
   `2770`, all files `660` — **including files that are already
   there** (show the one command that fixes existing files).
3. Prove inheritance: create a new file in `clean/data/`, show it
   landed group-`analysts`, group-writable.
4. In `clean/README.md`, add a "Permissions" section explaining your
   mode choices and what a teammate from *outside* the group can and
   cannot do, verified by one `sudo -u nobody test -r` style
   demonstration.

## Submission

`git init` in `~/a1-starter`, commit everything (no `add -A` for the
final commit — show explicit adds in the transcript), and submit the
repo plus `a1.log` (your full `script` transcript).

## Grading (100 pts → 5%)

| Area | Pts | Evidence required |
|---|---|---|
| Inventory pipeline | 15 | pipelines visible, numbers plausible |
| Reorganization | 15 | tree matches spec, no deletions |

| Duplicate detection | 10 | content-level, not name-level |
